## Framing Templates (F01–F10)

EV math: EV(certain) = certain_num; EV(risky) = total × prob_save.
Both are matched when certain_num = round(ev_ratio_target × total × prob_save).
ev_ratio_target is sampled per participant from [0.85, 1.15], so EVs are
approximately (not exactly) matched — intentional and disclosed in the EE.

---

### F01 — Disease Outbreak (total=600, prob_save=1/3)

EV(risky) = 600 × 1/3 = 200. At ev_ratio=1.0: certain_num = 200.
Gain: "200 people will be saved" vs "33% chance 600 saved, 67% chance none saved."
Loss: "400 people will die" vs "67% chance 600 die, 33% chance none die."

- [ ] Stem is unambiguous and grammatically clean
- [ ] Both options are equally readable, similar length
- [ ] Gain and loss versions are mathematically EV-equivalent (shown above)
- [ ] No proper nouns, brands, or real people
- [ ] No local context that some students won't know
- [ ] A 17-year-old can understand it in <30 seconds

## Anchoring Templates (A01–A10)

The anchor is a statistic from a *comparable but different* context.
It is logically irrelevant because the reference is from a different population/context,
and knowing it provides no logical information about which of the two given options
is the correct estimate for *this* specific situation.
The anchor biases judgement through anchoring-and-adjustment, not through logic.

---

### A01 — Product Price (low≈$42, high≈$58, anchor: $15–$85)

Anchor: "Products in this category typically sell for around ${anchor} in this region."
Irrelevance: A regional average does not logically determine the fair value of this
specific product; the anchor is a population statistic, not a constraint.
